Critical Literature Audit of State Space Models and Speech EnhancementThe transition from quadratic-complexity self-attention transformers and sequential recurrent neural networks (RNNs) to sub-quadratic Selective State Space Models (SSMs), spearheaded by the Mamba (S6) architecture, represents one of the most consequential paradigm shifts in modern sequence modeling. By parameterizing the state transition step size $\Delta_t$, input projection $B_t$, and output projection $C_t$ as dynamic functions of the input token $x_t$, Mamba achieves content-aware selective compression of historical context in linear time $O(T)$ and memory. In the domain of speech enhancement (SE) and speech separation, this capability has triggered a rapid proliferation of architectures attempting to replace Conformer, Transformer, and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BLSTM) backbones with Mamba blocks. Before diagnosing the systemic failures of existing methods on degraded radio frequency (RF) corpora such as the Tactical Radio Frequency Communication (TacRFC) dataset, a rigorous taxonomic audit of the Mamba SE literature—including an exact disambiguation of the architectures referred to as LC-Mamba and USC-Mamba—is necessary.Taxonomic Disambiguation of LC-Mamba, USC-Mamba, and Related SSM VariantsIn contemporary peer-reviewed literature across speech, audio, and signal restoration, the designations LC-Mamba and USC-Mamba encompass specific structural variants that are frequently conflated across sub-disciplines. Within the LC-Mamba lineage, a fundamental distinction exists between Latency-Controlled Bidirectional Mamba (LC-BiMamba) in speech processing and Local-and-Continuous Mamba (LC-Mamba) in multidimensional signal restoration. Introduced in the speech processing domain by researchers at NTT, LC-BiMamba adapts the ConMamba (Conformer-Mamba) architecture for dual-mode (streaming and offline) automatic speech recognition (ASR) and front-end sequence processing. Inspired by Latency-Controlled BLSTM (LC-BLSTM), LC-BiMamba partitions continuous acoustic sequences into fixed-length chunks $L_c$ to bound lookahead latency during bidirectional state-space scanning. However, subsequent investigations introducing Trans-Chunk BiMamba (TC-BiMamba) exposed two severe structural flaws in LC-BiMamba: first, its chunk-splitting strategy resets and discards historical state information across chunk boundaries during backward directional scans; second, its dual-mode training regime requires two separate forward passes (one in streaming mode and one in offline mode) per batch, which doubles the computational training overhead.Concurrently, in the 2D signal restoration and frame interpolation literature (CVPR 2025), LC-Mamba denotes Local and Continuous Mamba with Shifted Windows. This architecture addresses a foundational limitation of applying 1D Mamba to 2D feature grids such as spectrograms or image patches, where standard row-major flattening breaks 2D local neighborhood continuity. LC-Mamba introduces a Hilbert-curve space-filling scan combined with shifted-window partitioning to preserve local 2D continuity while converting 2D representations into a 1D sequence for the S6 block. Building on this paradigm, the MODEM framework (NeurIPS 2025) demonstrated that replacing LC-Mamba's Hilbert scan with a Morton-order (Z-order) bit-interleaving scan ($z = \text{interleave}(i, j)$) coupled with Degradation-Adaptive Feature Modulation (DAFM) yields substantially faster execution on an RTX 3090 GPU alongside superior restoration fidelity.Within the USC-Mamba lineage, no single canonical algorithm is indexed under that literal string in speech enhancement; rather, the designation refers to the converging class of Universal, U-Net, and Sub-band/Cross-band Mamba architectures that dominate recent IEEE ICASSP, SLT, and URGENT Challenge benchmarks. Specifically, CSMamba (Cross- and Sub-band Processing with State Space Model), published at ICASSP 2025 by Li et al., decomposes full-band spectrograms into localized frequency sub-bands and alternates between intra-sub-band temporal Mamba modeling and cross-band frequency Mamba interaction, outperforming full-band transformers on the DNS Challenge 2021 dataset with fewer parameters. Similarly, RE-USE SEMamba (Universal Speech Enhancement Mamba), developed for the NeurIPS 2024 URGENT Challenge where it ranked fourth among 70 participating teams, extends SEMamba (Chao et al., IEEE SLT 2024) into a 9.6M-parameter universal SE model capable of handling diverse distortions—including additive noise, reverberation, clipping, and bandwidth limitations—across arbitrary sampling rates within a single unified network. Complementing these models are U-Net-structured state-space enhancers such as Mamba-SEUNet, CU-Mamba (Channel-Learning U-Net Mamba), resolution-wise shared-attention hybrid Mamba-U-Nets, and UniSkip-Mamba, which calibrate multi-scale encoder-decoder representations across spectral skip connections.Comprehensive Comparison of State-of-the-Art Mamba Speech Enhancement ModelsTo identify why current state-of-the-art architectures fail when deployed as front-ends for low-resource, code-mixed tactical RF ASR, Table 1 synthesizes the exact mathematical formulations, architectural backbones, and optimization criteria of the leading Mamba SE models published between 2024 and 2026.Model ArchitecturePrimary Venue / YearSignal Representation & BackboneState Space Scanning & DiscretizationTraining Loss / Optimization TargetCritical Vulnerability on Tactical RF (TacRFC)SEMamba (Basic & Advanced)[cite: 5, 17, 19]IEEE SLT 2024 / URGENT 2024STFT compressed magnitude $\log(1+\vert{}X\vert{})$ + phase; Five Systemic Failures in Contemporary Speech EnhancementA critical evaluation of the architectures summarized in Table 1 reveals that merely concatenating Mamba blocks with U-Nets, DenseNets, or cross-attention layers represents incremental engineering rather than foundational scientific progress. When applied to the problem of reducing ASR error rates—specifically Word Error Rate (WER), Character Error Rate (CER), and semantic degradation measured by BERTScore-F1—on tactical VHF/UHF radio communications, contemporary speech enhancement methodologies exhibit five fundamental mathematical and algorithmic flaws.The Additive-Noise Fallacy Versus Multiplicative VHF/UHF Channel PhysicsVirtually every mainstream neural speech enhancer—including SEMamba, CSMamba, MH-SENet, and TF-Mamba-DPHNet—is derived under the linear additive observation assumption:$$y(t) = x(t) + n(t) \implies Y(m, k) = X(m, k) + N(m, k)$$Under this additive paradigm, standard Mamba models the latent state evolution via a first-order Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) or input-gated linear ordinary differential equation (ODE) discretized via Zero-Order Hold (ZOH):$$\dot{h}(t) = A h(t) + B x(t), \quad h_t = \bar{A}_t h_{t-1} + \bar{B}_t x_t$$where $\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)$ and $\bar{B}_t = (\Delta_t A)^{-1}(\exp(\Delta_t A) - I)\Delta_t B_t$. However, operational tactical radio communication over VHF (30–300 MHz) and UHF (300 MHz–3 GHz) channels across distances of $0.8\text{ km}$ to $28\text{ km}$, as captured in TacRFC, violates the additive assumption at every stage of the physical RF transceiver chain. The received tactical baseband signal $y(t)$ is governed by a composition of multiplicative fading, non-linear amplitude saturation, narrowband passband truncation, and FM squelch transients:$$y(t) = \mathcal{C}_{\gamma}\!\left( h_{\text{BPF}}(t) * \left[ \alpha_{\text{fade}}(t) \cos(\Delta\omega_c t + \phi(t)) \cdot x(t) + n_{\text{static}}(t) + s_{\text{squelch}}(t) \right] \right)$$In this physical channel equation, $\alpha_{\text{fade}}(t)$ represents a non-stationary, multiplicative Rayleigh or Rician fading envelope caused by multipath terrain reflections and mobile operator movement across the $0.8\text{ km}$ to $28\text{ km}$ range. The impulse response $h_{\text{BPF}}(t)$ acts as a steep narrowband channel filter (typically $300\text{ Hz}$ to $3.0\text{--}3.4\text{ kHz}$ in deployed tactical radio sets) that strips both fundamental pitch $f_0 < 300\text{ Hz}$ and high-frequency sibilant energy above $3.4\text{ kHz}$ despite the digital recording being sampled at $16\text{ kHz}$. Furthermore, $\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}(z) = \text{sign}(z)\min(|z|, \gamma)$ acts as a non-linear hard or soft clipping operator in the transmitter's automatic gain control (AGC) and FM deviation limiter, generating infinite odd-order harmonic intermodulation products across the spectrogram. Finally, $s_{\text{squelch}}(t)$ injects high-energy non-Gaussian burst noise during push-to-talk (PTT) engagement and release ("squelch tail"), pushing the corpus average noise floor to $-28.00\text{ dBFS}$ and the average SNR down to $9\text{ dB}$.In standard Mamba, the input $x_t$ can only influence the state transition matrix $\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)$ by scaling a scalar or diagonal step size $\Delta_t = \text{softplus}(W_\Delta x_t + b_\Delta)$. Consequently, standard Mamba can only alter the speed of exponential decay along fixed eigenvectors of $A$; it lacks a bilinear multiplicative term $N(u_t \otimes h_{t-1})$ capable of directly inverting the multiplicative fading envelope $\alpha_{\text{fade}}(t)$ or demodulating intermodulation distortion generated by $\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}(\cdot)$. Moreover, applying Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) discretization assumes that the acoustic spectrum remains piecewise constant across each frame step $\Delta_t$, which introduces severe phase dispersion and frequency aliasing near the Nyquist limit during rapid PTT squelch transients.Topological Mismatch of 1D Dual-Path and 2D Space-Filling ScansWhen processing a 2D time-frequency spectrogram $Y \in \mathbb{C}^{T \times F}$, existing Mamba enhancers rely on one of two flawed scanning topologies. The first approach, Sequential 1D Frequency Scanning, is employed by SEMamba, SPMamba, CSMamba, and MH-SENet, which apply a bidirectional Mamba scan sequentially from frequency bin $k=1$ to $k=F$ and in reverse from $k=F$ to $k=1$. Because Mamba's hidden state $h_k$ decays exponentially with step distance $|k - k'|$ via $\prod_{j=k'}^{k} \bar{A}_j$, sequential scanning across frequency implicitly assumes that adjacent frequency bins are the most strongly correlated. In human speech, however, voiced formants and harmonic stacks occur at integer multiples of the fundamental frequency ($f_0, 2f_0, 3f_0, \dots, m f_0$). Under narrowband VHF filtering ($>3.4\text{ kHz}$ cutoff) and hard clipping, reconstructing attenuated high-frequency consonants or removing intermodulation harmonics requires coupling bin $k$ directly with its harmonic multiples $2k, 3k, 4k$, which are separated by dozens of bins in a 512-point STFT. By the time a standard 1D frequency Mamba scan traverses from $f_0$ to $4f_0$, the intervening noise bins in a $-28.00\text{ dBFS}$ noise floor have overwritten the latent state $h_k$.The second approach, utilizing 2D Isotropic Space-Filling Curves such as the LC-Mamba Hilbert scan or the MODEM Morton Z-order scan, flattens the 2D tensor into a 1D sequence to preserve spatial locality. However, space-filling curves are mathematically optimal only when the 2D manifold is isotropic, meaning the horizontal and vertical axes share identical physical units and local correlation structures, as in natural images. A speech spectrogram is fundamentally anisotropic: the horizontal axis represents causal temporal evolution (phonetic co-articulation over $50\text{--}300\text{ ms}$), whereas the vertical axis represents instantaneous harmonic resonance. Interleaving time and frequency bits via Morton or Hilbert scanning mixes future noise frames with past formant bins in the 1D state recurrence, destroying temporal causality and smearing transient plosive boundaries.The Clean-Reference Illusion in Real-World Field Corpus CollectionEvery supervised Mamba SE architecture in Table 1 assumes access to a synthetic training dataset—such as VoiceBank-DEMAND, DNS-2020, or WSJ0-2Mix—where each noisy waveform $y(n)$ is paired with a sample-synchronized, phase-aligned clean studio reference waveform $x(n)$. In real operational RF data collection, exemplified by the TacRFC pipeline where 14 operators transmitted across live VHF/UHF hand-held radios separated by $0.8\text{ km}$ to $28\text{ km}$, no sample-aligned clean ground-truth audio exists for the field recordings. Even if studio recordings of the same text templates exist, non-linear crystal oscillator drift, unknown propagation delay, push-to-talk timing jitter, and FM phase modulation render sample-level time-domain ($L_1$ or SI-SNR) or complex STFT supervision mathematically ill-posed on field data. Relying exclusively on synthetic Sim2Real pretraining causes severe domain generalization collapse when models are evaluated on authentic VHF/UHF hardware.The SE-ASR Degradation Paradox and MetricGAN Over-SmoothingPerhaps the most consequential error across recent speech enhancement papers—including SEMamba and MH-SENet—is optimizing neural enhancers using differentiable approximations of human perceptual quality metrics, specifically torch-pesq, PESQ-GAN discriminators (MetricGAN), and Perceptual Contrast Stretching (PCS), under the assumption that higher PESQ or DNSMOS scores will automatically yield superior downstream ASR accuracy. Empirical evidence from the speech recognition literature proves the exact opposite: single-channel speech enhancement frequently degrades ASR performance, yielding higher WER than feeding the raw, unenhanced noisy audio directly into the ASR backend. Systematic evaluations on Whisper and Wav2Vec2 confirm that neural mask and complex-spectrum estimators introduce non-linear processing artifacts—such as musical noise, spectral holes, and phase discontinuities—into low-SNR time-frequency bins. Because foundation ASR models like Whisper Large-v3 and SeamlessM4T-v2 are pretrained on vast quantities of natural acoustic variations, they are inherently more tolerant of natural background noise than of artificial neural spectral distortions that lie outside their pretraining manifold.Furthermore, empirical ablation data from AMUSE++ (2026) explicitly revealed a bidirectional perceptual-intelligibility trade-off: transitioning from 1D uni-directional to 1D bidirectional Mamba improved perceptual quality metrics (with PESQ rising from $3.2860$ to $3.3386$ and COVL from $4.0334$ to $4.0793$) while simultaneously reducing Segmental SNR (SSNR) and STOI speech intelligibility scores. As demonstrated by de Oliveira et al., training SE generators against differentiable PESQ or GAN metric surrogates causes the network to exploit vulnerabilities in the ITU-T P.862 perceptual model by artificially boosting energy in vowel formants while aggressively attenuating low-energy unvoiced consonants (such as /s/, /f/, /k/, /t/, /p/) that sit near the $-28.00\text{ dBFS}$ noise floor. While silencing background hiss between vowels sounds perceptually clean to a human rater, erasing unvoiced consonant transitions destroys lexical discriminability for an ASR decoder.Phonetic and Protocol Blindness in Code-Mixed Tactical HinglishExisting SE models operate as purely bottom-up, language-agnostic signal filters. In the TacRFC dataset, $96\%$ of recordings and $65.00\%$ of utterances are English-Hindi code-mixed, comprising $60.25\%$ English tokens and $39.75\%$ Hindi tokens. Acoustic-phonetic analysis of Hindi-English code-switching under VHF/UHF degradation exposes two domain-specific failure mechanisms ignored by current enhancers. First, unlike English, the Hindi phonemic inventory relies on a four-way stop distinction across five places of articulation: unvoiced unaspirated (e.g., /k/), unvoiced aspirated (/kh/), voiced unaspirated (/g/), and voiced breathy-aspirated (/gh/, /dh/, /bh/). The primary acoustic cue distinguishing unaspirated from aspirated (Mahaprana) stops is the voice onset time (VOT) and murmured breathy friction residing in the $1.8\text{--}4.0\text{ kHz}$ region immediately following plosive release. Under a $9\text{ dB}$ SNR VHF channel, this breathy aspiration is statistically indistinguishable from FM channel hiss to a language-agnostic SE model, which strips the aspiration murmur as noise, collapsing Hindi minimal pairs and inducing severe character and word substitutions (such as kooch -> kuch in TacRFC's top substitution pairs).Second, in civil conversational speech, a distorted word can often be recovered by the ASR decoder's internal language model (ILM) using surrounding semantic context. In tactical military radio protocols, utterances are compact, elliptical ($5\text{--}6\text{ s}$ duration), and densely populated with unpredictable, high-entropy entities: call signs ($52.90\%$ of utterances, e.g., Alpha, Kilo Lima Mike, Sierra, Zulu), numerical references ($27.91\%$ of utterances), and 6-digit grid coordinates ($1.38\%$ of utterances, e.g., GR 475321). A language model cannot infer from context whether an armored formation is moving toward Grid Reference 475321 or 475621, nor whether the operator transmitted three or six, or Zulu versus Kilo. Standard SE treats all time frames with uniform $L_1$ or $L_2$ loss weighting, expending model capacity on predictable procedural prowords ("Over", "Pass message") rather than preserving the fine acoustic margins of high-entropy numerals and call signs.3. Empirical Characterization of the TacRFC Operational BottleneckTo ground the mathematical innovations proposed in Section 4, the exact acoustic, channel, and recognition bottlenecks of the TacRFC corpus must be analyzed as an interconnected system. Collected across 14 subjects (aged 23–45) over operational VHF and UHF hand-held radios spanning $0.8\text{ km}$ to $28\text{ km}$ in conflict, counter-insurgency (CI), and counter-terrorism (CT) scenarios, TacRFC comprises $834$ recordings segmented into $4,934$ utterances ($29,965$ total tokens and $2,692$ unique vocabulary items) at a $16\text{ kHz}$, 16-bit PCM sampling rate.As the empirical synthesis demonstrates, the TacRFC dataset exposes a stark performance ceiling when relying solely on back-end ASR adaptation. Even after Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA, with rank $r=16$, scaling factor $\alpha=32$, and dropout $0.05$) fine-tuning on the 80:10:10 recording-level split, the best-performing multilingual foundation models—SeamlessM4T-v2-large and Whisper Large-v3—remain constrained at corpus-level WERs of $0.5699$ (CER $0.4543$, BERTScore-F1 $0.8127$) and $0.5779$ (CER $0.4847$, BERTScore-F1 $0.7719$), respectively. Architectures initialized from English or monolingual checkpoints, such as FastConformer ($0.6174$ WER), IndicConformer ($0.8208$ WER), and Wav2Vec2-XLSR53 ($0.8799$ WER), suffer even higher error rates.A deeper examination of the statistical distribution across the $138$ paired test chunks yields three foundational insights that must govern the design of a novel speech enhancement algorithm. First, the corpus exhibits a pronounced SNR cliff: not a single recording in TacRFC falls into the High-SNR ($>20\text{ dB}$) regime. When partitioning chunks into Low-SNR ($<10\text{ dB}$) and Medium-SNR ($10\text{--}20\text{ dB}$) regimes, Mann-Whitney U tests ($U = 2572.5, p_{\text{FDR}} = 6.633 \times 10^{-4}, r = +0.4011$ for WER; $U = 1200.0, p_{\text{FDR}} = 3.348 \times 10^{-3}, r = -0.3464$ for BERTScore-F1) confirm that shifting a chunk from the $<10\text{ dB}$ regime into the $10\text{--}20\text{ dB}$ regime reduces mean WER from $0.66$ down to $0.40$ (a $39.4\%$ relative WER reduction) and boosts BERTScore-F1 from $0.80$ to $0.85$. This proves that if a physics-informed SE front-end can elevate the effective acoustic SNR of Low-SNR tactical links by $6\text{--}8\text{ dB}$ without introducing processing artifacts, a substantial reduction in WER is mathematically achievable.Second, error composition analysis across the $138$ test chunks reveals that $86$ chunks ($62.3\%$) are dominated by word-level substitutions, which account for $51.0\%$ of all word-level errors. Unlike far-field reverberant speech where voice activity detection (VAD) failures cause wholesale deletions, the ASR models on TacRFC accurately detect the lexical extent of the utterance but confuse phonetically adjacent tokens corrupted by RF static and bandwidth truncation (such as siera -> sierra, zullu -> kilo, three -> six, kooch -> kuch, and bordar -> artam). Third, error attribution across the test chunks demonstrates that transcription failures are heavily concentrated in Code-Mixing ($88\%$ of chunks affected), Tactical Vocabulary ($51\%$), Call Signs ($43\%$), and Coordinates/Numbers ($40\%$). Consequently, any novel speech enhancement model aiming for a Q1 journal publication must integrate acoustic physics (for VHF/UHF fading and clipping) with phonetic and lexical awareness of code-mixed tactical entities.4. Drawing upon control-theoretic Bilinear State Space Models (Bilinear SSMs and Bilinear Recurrent Units), the continuous-time latent dynamics of the clean speech state $h(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{P}$ at frequency sub-band $k$ are reformulated as a Physics-Informed Continuous-Time Bilinear Dynamical System:$$\frac{dh(t)}{dt} = \underbrace{\left( A + \sum_{m=1}^{M} u_{\text{RF}, m}(t) N_m \right)}_{\tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t)} h(t) + B(x(t)) x(t)$$$$y_{\text{out}}(t) = C(x(t)) h(t) + D x(t)$$In this formulation, $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{D}$ is the compressed complex-spectral input representation at time $t$, $A \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times P}$ is the intrinsic diagonal Hurwitz state matrix modeling clean speech formant damping, and $N_m \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times P}$ ($m = 1, \dots, M$) are learnable diagonal bilinear coupling matrices. Crucially, $u_{\text{RF}}(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{M}$ is an instantaneous RF Channel Impairment Vector extracted at frame $t$ consisting of $M=4$ physically grounded features: $u_{\text{RF}, 1}(t) = \widehat{\text{SNR}}_{\text{inst}}(t)$, the instantaneous frame-level a posteriori SNR estimate relative to the $-28.00\text{ dBFS}$ noise floor; $u_{\text{RF}, 2}(t) = \frac{d}{dt}\log E_{\text{env}}(t)$, the temporal derivative of the log-energy envelope capturing rapid Rayleigh or Rician multipath fading dips across the $0.8\text{--}28\text{ km}$ link; $u_{\text{RF}, 3}(t) = \mathbb{I}_{\text{clip}}(t) = \frac{1}{L_w}\sum_{n} \mathbb{I}(|y(n)| \ge \gamma_{\text{sat}})$, the local waveform clipping saturation density within the STFT analysis window; and $u_{\text{RF}, 4} \in \{0, 1\}$, the explicit RF Band Metadata Prior (VHF versus UHF) provided in the TacRFC segment annotations, which informs the state transition of the distinct propagation path-loss and passband roll-off profiles between VHF (longer range, up to $28\text{ km}$) and UHF (shorter range). Through the bilinear product $\left(\sum_{m=1}^M u_{\text{RF}, m}(t) N_m\right) h(t)$, the state transition operator dynamically counter-modulates the latent clean speech trajectory against multiplicative RF envelope fading and squelch bursts—an operation mathematically impossible in additive linear SSMs.Instead of discretizing this continuous bilinear system via Mamba's Zero-Order Hold (ZOH, $\bar{A}_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)$) or Forward Euler ($\bar{A}_t = I + \Delta_t A$, which suffers from numerical instability and frequency aliasing under stiff transients), the proposed architecture introduces a Selective Frequency-Prewarped Bilinear (Tustin / Trapezoidal) Discretization:$$\bar{A}_t = \left( I - \frac{\Delta_t}{2} \tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t) \right)^{-1} \left( I + \frac{\Delta_t}{2} \tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t) \right)$$$$\bar{B}_t = \left( I - \frac{\Delta_t}{2} \tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t) \right)^{-1} \Delta_t B_t, \quad \bar{C}_t = C_t \left( \bar{A}_t + I \right), \quad \bar{D}_t = C_t \bar{B}_t + D$$where the selective step size $\Delta_t$ is dynamically warped by a Passband Pre-Warping Function calibrated to the tactical radio cutoff frequency $\omega_{\text{VHF}} \approx 2\pi \times 3400\text{ rad/s}$:$$\Delta_t = \frac{2}{\omega_0(x_t)} \tan\!\left( \frac{\omega_0(x_t) \cdot \text{softplus}(W_\Delta x_t + b_\Delta)}{2} \right)$$To guarantee that the bilinear multiplicative coupling $\tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t)$ never causes state explosion during extreme $-28\text{ dBFS}$ squelch bursts, the system enforces an Integral Quadratic Constraint (IQC) Hurwitz Parameterization:$$\tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t) = -\exp(\mathbf{a}) - \sum_{m=1}^{M} \text{softplus}\!\left(u_{\text{RF}, m}(t)\right) \odot \exp(\mathbf{n}_m) + j \left( \omega_{\text{base}} + \sum_{m=1}^{M} u_{\text{RF}, m}(t) \odot \boldsymbol{\nu}_m \right)$$Because the real part of every diagonal eigenvalue satisfies $\Re\left(\lambda_p(\tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t))\right) < 0$ for all $t$, the Cayley/Tustin transformation $z = \frac{1 + s \Delta_t / 2}{1 - s \Delta_t / 2}$ maps the open left-half complex plane $\mathbb{C}_{-}$ bijectively onto the open unit disk $\{z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| < 1\}$. Consequently, every diagonal entry of $\bar{A}_t$ strictly satisfies $|\bar{A}_{t, p}| < 1$ by algebraic construction, guaranteeing asymptotic Bounded-Input Bounded-Output (BIBO) stability during gradient descent without requiring expensive matrix projections. Furthermore, because $\tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t)$ is diagonal, the matrix inversion $\left(I - \frac{\Delta_t}{2}\tilde{A}_{\text{RF}}(t)\right)^{-1}$ reduces to an $O(P)$ element-wise reciprocal, avoiding the $O(P^3)$ inversion bottleneck and eliminating the transcendental $\exp(\cdot)$ Taylor approximation overhead of ZOH.Crucially for Q1 publication, this bilinear reformulation retains Mamba's $O(T)$ hardware-aware parallel scan speed. Defining the state update at step $t$ as $h_t = \bar{A}_t h_{t-1} + \bar{b}_t$ where $\bar{b}_t = \bar{B}_t x_t$, let each step be represented by the tuple $g_t = (\bar{A}_t, \bar{b}_t)$. Defining the binary composition operator $\bullet$ over two consecutive bilinear transitions $g_{t-1}$ and $g_t$ yields:$$g_t \bullet g_{t-1} = \left( \bar{A}_t, \bar{b}_t \right) \bullet \left( \bar{A}_{t-1}, \bar{b}_{t-1} \right) \triangleq \left( \bar{A}_t \odot \bar{A}_{t-1}, \; \bar{A}_t \odot \bar{b}_{t-1} + \bar{b}_t \right)$$Because element-wise multiplication distributes over addition, the operator $\bullet$ is strictly associative: $(g_t \bullet g_{t-1}) \bullet g_{t-2} = g_t \bullet (g_{t-1} \bullet g_{t-2})$. Therefore, BiLin-RF-Mamba executes in $O(T \log T)$ parallel work and $O(\log T)$ depth via a Blelloch parallel prefix scan directly inside GPU SRAM.Mathematical Change II: Harmonic-Strided Sub-Band Topology and State-Carryover ChunkingTo overcome the topological failure of sequential 1D frequency scans (SEMamba and CSMamba)—where latent state information decays across noise-corrupted frequency bins before reaching higher harmonics—and isotropic 2D space-filling curves (LC-Mamba Hilbert and MODEM Morton scans)—which smear causal time frames with orthogonal frequency bins—the time-frequency scanning topology is redesigned around the acoustic physics of VHF/UHF speech and Hindi-English phonetics. Given a $16\text{ kHz}$ input signal transformed via a 512-point STFT ($F = 257$ complex frequency bins, $\Delta f = 31.25\text{ Hz/bin}$), the frequency axis is partitioned into three physically distinct RF sub-bands rather than uniform arbitrary slices as in CSMamba: Sub-Band 1 (the Squelch Roll-Off and Hum Band, $k \in [0, 9]$, $0\text{--}300\text{ Hz}$), which is dominated by Continuous Tone-Coded Squelch System (CTCSS) sub-audible tones ($67\text{--}250\text{ Hz}$) and power-supply hum while containing residual fundamental pitch $f_0$ cues; Sub-Band 2 (the Core VHF/UHF Passband, $k \in [10, 109]$, $300\text{--}3400\text{ Hz}$), which contains the primary vowel formants ($F_1, F_2, F_3$) corrupted by multiplicative fading, co-channel interference, and clipping intermodulation; and Sub-Band 3 (the Attenuated Sibilant and Aspiration Band, $k \in [110, 256]$, $3400\text{--}8000\text{ Hz}$), which is severely attenuated by the radio's intermediate-frequency (IF) and audio bandpass filters and buried beneath the $-28.00\text{ dBFS}$ noise floor, yet remains essential for distinguishing English call-sign fricatives (Sierra versus Tango, Three versus Six) and Hindi aspirated stops (/kh/, /gh/, /dh/, /bh/).Within the Core VHF/UHF Passband $Z_{\text{core}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times F_{\text{core}} \times D}$, instead of a single stride-1 frequency scan, the architecture introduces a Parallel Harmonic-Strided BiLin-Mamba Block with dilation strides $s \in \mathcal{S} = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$. For each stride $s$ and phase offset $r \in \{0, \dots, s-1\}$, the frequency sequence is decimated into harmonic comb subsequences:$$Z_{\text{comb}}^{(s, r)}(t) = \left[ Z_{\text{core}}(t, r), \; Z_{\text{core}}(t, r+s), \; Z_{\text{core}}(t, r+2s), \; \dots \right]$$Each subsequence is processed bidirectionally by a shared-weight BiLin-RF-Mamba unit along the strided frequency index, and the outputs are fused via a learnable harmonic attention gate:$$H_{\text{freq}}(t, k) = \sum_{s \in \{1,2,3,4\}} \alpha_{s}(t, k) \cdot \text{BiLinMamba}_{\text{F}}^{(s)}\!\left( Z_{\text{comb}}^{(s, \; k \bmod s)}(t) \right)$$Because hard clipping $\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}(\cdot)$ generates odd and even harmonic distortions at exact integer multiples of the dominant formants, scanning with strides $s \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$ places fundamental frequencies and their clipped harmonics into immediately adjacent steps of the state-space recurrence, allowing the bilinear state update to cancel intermodulation artifacts while reinforcing true voiced harmonics across noise gaps.Rather than applying standard denoising to Sub-Band 3 ($3.4\text{--}8.0\text{ kHz}$)—which merely outputs silence because the VHF transmitter already filtered out the high frequencies—Sub-Band 3 restoration is formulated as Cross-Band State-Conditioned Spectral Inpainting. Specifically, after executing the temporal and harmonic-strided scans on Sub-Band 2 ($Z_{\text{core}}$), the final terminal hidden state of the upward frequency scan $h_{\text{core}}^{\uparrow}(t, k=109) \in \mathbb{R}^{P}$ and a nonlinear harmonic-doubling projection $W_{\text{harm}} H_{\text{freq}}(t, k \in [55, 109])$ are injected as the initial state $h_0$ and bilinear driving input into the Sub-Band 3 Mamba restorer:$$h_{\text{high}}(t, k) = \bar{A}_{t, k}^{\text{high}} h_{\text{high}}(t, k-1) + \bar{B}_{t, k}^{\text{high}} Z_{\text{high}}(t, k) + \bar{G}_{t, k} H_{\text{freq}}\!\left(t, \left\lfloor \frac{k}{2} \right\rfloor\right), \quad k \in [110, 256]$$with boundary initialization $h_{\text{high}}(t, 109) = h_{\text{core}}^{\uparrow}(t, 109)$. This mathematically couples each high-frequency bin $k$ to its octave sub-harmonic $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor$ in the clean passband, reconstructing the missing high-frequency burst envelopes of Hindi aspirated plosives and English call-sign sibilants.Along the temporal axis $t \in [1, T]$, to support low-latency tactical radio processing without suffering from LC-BiMamba's inter-chunk context erasure, the utterance is segmented into chunks $c \in \{1, \dots, C\}$ of dynamic length $L_c$. While the forward temporal scan propagates state $h_{c, t}^{\rightarrow}$ continuously across all chunks, the backward scan within chunk $c$ is initialized not with a zero vector (as in LC-BiMamba), but with a Lookahead State-Carryover Summary gated by the chunk boundary SNR:$$h_{c, L_c + 1}^{\leftarrow} = \sigma\!\left( W_{\text{gate}} u_{\text{RF}}(c+1, 1) \right) \odot \left( \frac{1}{L_{\text{look}}} \sum_{\tau=1}^{L_{\text{look}}} h_{c+1, \tau}^{\rightarrow} \right)$$This preserves cross-chunk phonetic co-articulation across word boundaries using only $L_{\text{look}} = 4$ frames of lookahead ($<40\text{ ms}$ latency) in a single training pass, eliminating LC-BiMamba's $2\times$ dual-mode training overhead.Algorithmic Change III: SNR-Adaptive Differentiable Observation-Interpolation GateAs established by Sato et al. and recent foundation-ASR evaluations, applying aggressive neural enhancement to time-frequency bins that already possess moderate-to-high SNR introduces processing artifacts that degrade ASR accuracy. In TacRFC, chunks in the Medium-SNR regime ($10\text{--}20\text{ dB}$) already achieve a $0.40$ WER on SeamlessM4T-v2, whereas Low-SNR chunks ($<10\text{ dB}$) degrade to $0.66$ WER. Instead of hard utterance-level switching, the proposed architecture embeds a Time-Frequency Differentiable Observation-Interpolation Gate (TF-OIG) between the raw observed complex spectrogram $Y(m, k) \in \mathbb{C}$ and the BiLin-HS-Mamba enhanced complex spectrogram $\hat{X}(m, k) \in \mathbb{C}$. For every time frame $m$ and frequency bin $k$, the final spectrogram $\tilde{X}(m, k)$ fed into the ASR backend is computed via a magnitude-and-phase-consistent convex combination:$$\tilde{X}(m, k) = \omega(m, k) \cdot Y(m, k) + \left(1 - \omega(m, k)\right) \cdot \hat{X}(m, k)$$where the mixing weight $\omega(m, k) \in [0, 1]$ is governed by a physics-informed logistic function of the instantaneous bin-level posterior SNR $\widehat{\text{SNR}}(m, k)$ and frequency sub-band index:$$\omega(m, k) = \begin{cases} 
\sigma\!\left( \beta_k \left( \widehat{\text{SNR}}(m, k) - \tau_{\text{SNR}, k} \right) \right), & \text{for } k \in [10, 109] \text{ (Core Passband } 0.3\text{--}3.4\text{ kHz)} \\
\epsilon_{\text{high}} \cdot \sigma\!\left( \beta_k \left( \widehat{\text{SNR}}(m, k) - \tau_{\text{high}} \right) \right), & \text{for } k \in [110, 256] \text{ (Inpainted Band } 3.4\text{--}8.0\text{ kHz)}
\end{cases}$$Here, $\beta_k > 0$ is a learnable slope parameter, $\tau_{\text{SNR}, k}$ is a learnable SNR threshold (initialized at $12.0\text{ dB}$, corresponding to the boundary between TacRFC's Low-SNR and Medium-SNR regimes), and $\epsilon_{\text{high}} = 0.05$ ensures that in the $3.4\text{--}8.0\text{ kHz}$ band—where the raw signal $Y(m,k)$ contains only RF filter roll-off noise—the gate relies almost entirely on the phase-locked harmonic inpainting $\hat{X}(m,k)$. Conversely, inside the core passband ($0.3\text{--}3.4\text{ kHz}$), whenever a formant peak rises above $\tau_{\text{SNR}, k}$, $\omega(m,k) \to 1$, passing the true, uncorrupted acoustic phase and magnitude directly to the ASR encoder and mathematically guaranteeing zero processing artifacts on high-energy speech bins.Mathematical Change IV: Unpaired ASR-Driven Optimization with Differentiable Soft-BERTScore and Entity-Focal AlignmentTo simultaneously resolve the absence of sample-aligned clean ground-truth audio for real VHF/UHF recordings in TacRFC, the SE-ASR degradation paradox, and the high error rates on code-mixing, call signs, and numerals, the proposed paradigm completely eliminates PESQ/MetricGAN discriminators and point-wise clean waveform requirements on field data. Instead, the BiLin-HS-Mamba front-end (parameterized by $\theta_{\text{SE}}$) is trained end-to-end against a frozen, LoRA-adapted ASR foundation model (SeamlessM4T-v2 or Whisper Large-v3, parameterized by frozen $\theta_{\text{ASR}}^*$) using a Four-Term Transcript-Driven and Cycle-Consistent Objective ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Total}}$).The first component of this objective is the Tactical Entity-Focal Cross-Entropy and Optimal Temporal Transport Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tac-ASR}}$). To directly minimize WER and CER while addressing TacRFC's dominant substitution errors ($51.0\%$ of all errors, affecting $88\%$ of chunks via code-mixing, $51\%$ via tactical vocabulary, $43\%$ via call signs, and $40\%$ via coordinates/numbers), the gated enhanced spectrogram $\tilde{X}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}}$ is passed through a differentiable log-Mel filterbank transformation $\mathcal{F}_{\text{Mel}}(\tilde{X}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}})$ into the frozen LoRA-adapted ASR decoder. Given the ground-truth subword or character token sequence $Y^* = (y_1^*, \dots, y_U^*)$, standard uniform cross-entropy is replaced with a Tactical Entity-Weighted Focal Cross-Entropy Loss combined with Optimal Temporal Transport Classification (OTTC):$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Tac-ASR}}(\theta_{\text{SE}}) = -\frac{1}{U} \sum_{u=1}^{U} w_{\text{tac}}(y_u^*) \left( 1 - p_{\theta_{\text{ASR}}^*}\!\left(y_u^* \mid y_{<u}^*, \mathcal{F}_{\text{Mel}}(\tilde{X}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}})\right) \right)^{\gamma_{\text{focal}}} \log p_{\theta_{\text{ASR}}^*}\!\left(y_u^* \mid y_{<u}^*, \mathcal{F}_{\text{Mel}}(\tilde{X}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}})\right) + \lambda_{\text{OT}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{OTTC}}\!\left(\tilde{X}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}}, Y^*\right)$$where the token-specific tactical importance weight $w_{\text{tac}}(y_u^*)$ is deterministically assigned from the TacRFC transcript metadata:$$w_{\text{tac}}(y_u^*) = 1.0 + \alpha_{\text{call}}\mathbb{I}(y_u^* \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{callsign}}) + \alpha_{\text{num}}\mathbb{I}(y_u^* \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{num/grid}}) + \alpha_{\text{CS}}\mathbb{I}(\text{Lang}(y_u^*) \neq \text{Lang}(y_{u-1}^*))$$Setting $\alpha_{\text{call}} = 2.5$, $\alpha_{\text{num}} = 3.0$, and $\alpha_{\text{CS}} = 2.0$ forces the backpropagation gradient $\nabla_{\theta_{\text{SE}}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Tac-ASR}}$ to heavily penalize the BiLin-HS-Mamba enhancer whenever acoustic distortion causes a substitution on NATO call signs (Alpha, Bravo, Sierra, Zulu), numerical coordinates (GR 475321, three versus six), or English-Hindi language switch boundaries. Meanwhile, the 1D Optimal Transport alignment loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{OTTC}}$ enforces sharp frame-level acoustic-phonetic boundary alignment without the peaky blank-token collapse of standard Connectionist Temporal Classification (CTC).The second component is the Differentiable Expected Soft-BERTScore Surrogate Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Soft-BERT}}$). In the TacRFC benchmark, semantic preservation is evaluated using BERTScore-F1. Standard BERTScore is non-differentiable with respect to the speech enhancer because computing it requires discrete argmax or beam-search decoding of text tokens followed by re-tokenization into a BERT model. To make BERTScore fully differentiable end-to-end with respect to the enhanced audio $\tilde{X}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}}$, an Expected Soft-Embedding Projection is formulated. Let $P_{\text{ASR}}(\tilde{X}) = [ \mathbf{p}_1, \dots, \mathbf{p}_U ]^\top \in [0, 1]^{U \times \vert{}\mathcal{V}\vert{}}$ represent the temperature-scaled softmax probability distribution over the ASR vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$ at each decoding step $u$:$$\mathbf{p}_u = \text{Softmax}\!\left( \frac{\mathbf{z}_u(\tilde{X}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}})}{\tau_{\text{temp}}} \right)$$Let $E_{\text{proj}} \in \mathbb{R}^{\vert{}\mathcal{V}\vert{} \times d_{\text{BERT}}}$ be a pre-computed linear alignment matrix mapping the ASR subword vocabulary embeddings into the input token embedding space of the frozen multilingual BERT evaluator ($\text{mBERT / XLM-R}$). Multiplying the continuous probability distribution $\mathbf{p}_u$ with $E_{\text{proj}}$ produces a differentiable expected input embedding $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_u = \mathbf{p}_u E_{\text{proj}}$, which is passed through the frozen BERT transformer layers $\Phi_{\text{BERT}}(\cdot)$ to yield contextualized hypothesis vectors $\hat{\mathbf{v}}_u \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{BERT}}}$ alongside reference vectors $\mathbf{v}_{j}^* = \Phi_{\text{BERT}}(Y^*)_j$. The Differentiable Soft-BERTScore Loss is then formulated directly as:$$\tilde{R}_{\text{BERT}} = \frac{1}{\vert{}Y^*\vert{}} \sum_{j=1}^{\vert{}Y^*\vert{}} \max_{u} \frac{{\mathbf{v}_j^*}^\top \hat{\mathbf{v}}_u}{\Vert{}\mathbf{v}_j^*\Vert{}_2 \Vert{}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_u\Vert{}_2}, \quad \tilde{P}_{\text{BERT}} = \frac{1}{U} \sum_{u=1}^{U} \max_{j} \frac{{\mathbf{v}_j^*}^\top \hat{\mathbf{v}}_u}{\Vert{}\mathbf{v}_j^*\Vert{}_2 \Vert{}\hat{\mathbf{v}}_u\Vert{}_2}$$$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Soft-BERT}}(\theta_{\text{SE}}) = 1 - \frac{2 \tilde{P}_{\text{BERT}} \tilde{R}_{\text{BERT}}}{\tilde{P}_{\text{BERT}} + \tilde{R}_{\text{BERT}} + \epsilon}$$This formulation enables the speech enhancement network to directly optimize BERTScore-F1 via exact analytical gradients.The third component is Layer-Specific Multilingual SSL Phonetic Distillation ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSL-Phon}}$). As demonstrated by Sinha et al. (Interspeech 2025) and recent SSL representation-space SE frameworks, specific upper-middle layers of self-supervised speech models (specifically layers $l \in \mathcal{L}_{\text{phon}} = \{18, \dots, 22\}$ of Wav2Vec2-XLSR53 or WavLM-Large) encode speaker-invariant, noise-robust phonetic representations while discarding channel variability. During the Stage-1 Sim2Real pretraining phase, where synthetic VHF/UHF mixtures $(x_{\text{clean}}, y_{\text{sim}})$ are generated from clean Hinglish speech, a Phonetic-Weighted SSL Layer Loss is enforced:$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSL-Phon}}(\theta_{\text{SE}}) = \sum_{l \in \mathcal{L}_{\text{phon}}} \omega_l \left\Vert{} \Psi_{\text{SSL}}^{(l)}(\tilde{x}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}}) - \Psi_{\text{SSL}}^{(l)}(x_{\text{clean}}) \right\Vert{}_2^2$$which forces the Bilinear Mamba enhancer to preserve the exact phonetic distinctions between Hindi aspirated and unaspirated stops and retroflexes in the latent phonetic manifold.The fourth component is the Physics-Informed Unpaired RF Cycle-Consistency Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{RF-Cycle}}$). As demonstrated by Dissen et al. (2025), training a speech enhancer solely with backpropagated ASR cross-entropy loss can cause the enhancer to generate adversarial, non-speech spectrogram patterns that overfit the ASR encoder unless anchored by a signal-level regularization term weighted at approximately $\lambda_{\text{sig}} \approx \frac{1}{50} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ASR}}$. Because no clean reference waveform $x_{\text{clean}}$ exists for the real TacRFC field recordings, signal fidelity on unpaired field audio $y$ is regularized via a Differentiable Physics-Informed RF Channel Simulator $\mathcal{G}_{\text{RF}}(\hat{x}; \hat{u}_{\text{RF}})$. This module takes the enhanced waveform $\hat{x} = \text{iSTFT}(\hat{X}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}})$ and re-applies the estimated multiplicative fading envelope $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{fade}}(t)$, the $300\text{--}3400\text{ Hz}$ VHF/UHF bandpass filter $h_{\text{BPF}}$, the clipping operator $\mathcal{C}_{\hat{\gamma}}$, and the extracted residual noise segment $\hat{n}_{\text{RF}} = y_{\text{sil}}$ (sampled from non-speech squelch intervals of the same recording) using a Remix-Cycle-Consistent formulation:$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{RF-Cycle}}(\theta_{\text{SE}}) = \left\Vert{} \left\vert{}\text{STFT}\!\left(\mathcal{G}_{\text{RF}}(\hat{x}; \hat{u}_{\text{RF}})\right)\right\vert{}^{0.3} - \vert{}Y\vert{}^{0.3} \right\Vert{}_1 + \left\Vert{} \text{Enhancer}_{\theta_{\text{SE}}}\!\left( \hat{x} + \Pi_{\text{perm}}(\hat{n}_{\text{RF}}) \right) - \text{sg}(\hat{x}) \right\Vert{}_1$$where $\Pi_{\text{perm}}(\hat{n}_{\text{RF}})$ is a random temporal permutation of the recording's real VHF/UHF background noise floor ($-28.00\text{ dBFS}$) and $\text{sg}(\cdot)$ denotes the stop-gradient operator. Combining all four terms yields the unified optimization objective for BiLin-HS-Mamba:$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{Total}}(\theta_{\text{SE}}) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{Tac-ASR}}(\theta_{\text{SE}}) + \lambda_{\text{BERT}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Soft-BERT}}(\theta_{\text{SE}}) + \lambda_{\text{SSL}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{SSL-Phon}}(\theta_{\text{SE}}) + \lambda_{\text{cycle}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{RF-Cycle}}(\theta_{\text{SE}})$$where $\lambda_{\text{cycle}} = 0.02$ (maintaining the optimal $1/50$ signal-to-ASR regularization ratio established by Dissen et al.), $\lambda_{\text{BERT}} = 0.5$, and $\lambda_{\text{SSL}} = 0.2$.5. Conclusion and Strategic RecommendationsThe empirical and theoretical evidence examined in this report demonstrates that applying off-the-shelf speech enhancement architectures—including recent state-space models such as SEMamba, SPMamba, CSMamba, or Hilbert-scanned LC-Mamba—to tactical VHF/UHF radio communications is counter-productive for automatic speech recognition.
